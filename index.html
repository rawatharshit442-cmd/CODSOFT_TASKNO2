01 · KNOW THE THREAT

What is phishing?

Phishing attacks

Phishing is a social-engineering attack where an attacker impersonates a trusted person, service or organization to trick a user into revealing information or taking an unsafe action.

Remember

Urgency, fear, rewards, authority and curiosity are common psychological hooks. Pause before clicking.

02 · SPOT THE SIGNS

How to identify suspicious messages

✉ Suspicious email

  • Unexpected credential/payment request
  • Pressure to act immediately
  • Unknown attachment or link
  • Sender address does not match the claim
  • Odd wording or formatting

🔐 Fake login page

  • Look-alike or misspelled URL
  • Unexpected login prompt
  • Unusual domain
  • Reached from an unsolicited message
  • Requests unnecessary sensitive data

🌐 Fraudulent website

  • Offer seems too good to be true
  • Vague company details
  • Pressure to pay/share data
  • Unusual domain name
  • Copied branding or poor content

03 · SOCIAL ENGINEERING

Common techniques

Impersonation

Attacker pretends to be a manager, bank, delivery company or support agent.

Urgency & fear

The message claims your account will close or a payment is overdue unless you act now.

Reward bait

Prizes, refunds, discounts or exclusive offers trigger quick decisions.

Authority

A powerful-sounding role or organization is used to discourage questions.

Curiosity

A shocking document, photo or topic is used to make the victim click.

Pretexting

A believable story is invented to obtain information or persuade action.

04 · CASE STUDIES

Real-life scenarios

CASE 01

Fake account alert

An employee receives an email saying their account will be disabled in 30 minutes. A button leads to a look-alike login page.

Lesson

Do not use the email button. Open the official service through a known trusted route and verify the alert.

CASE 02

Fraudulent delivery message

A text claims a parcel cannot be delivered and asks for a small fee through a link.

Lesson

Check the delivery through the courier's official app or website instead of the message link.

CASE 03

Executive impersonation

A message appears to come from a senior employee and asks for urgent gift cards or money transfer.

Lesson

Verify unusual financial requests through a separate trusted communication channel.

05 · PRACTICAL DEFENSE

Stop. Check. Verify.

1Stop — Don't react to urgency.

2Inspect — Check sender, URL, context and request.

3Verify — Contact the organization using a trusted channel.

4Protect — Use MFA, unique passwords, updates and security tools.

5Report — Report suspected phishing through the approved process.
